# Moss Bay watercolor background study

Two original 1024 × 1536 watercolor-and-pencil canvases interpret an eye-level, generally west-facing view across Moss Bay from the water-facing pavilion area at Marina Park in Kirkland. They replace the earlier generic wooded-lake subject. Open water and a low distant urban/wooded shore replace the enclosing forest; the marina and long pier enter from the left, with a curved concrete waterfront edge and boulders near the viewer.

The exact standing position and camera bearing inside the pavilion are not verified. Public photos were inspected for broad geographic relationships. This is an informed portrait illustration, not a surveyed reconstruction. No complete pavilion is placed in front of the viewer, no large centered Seattle skyline is invented, and no unverified Mount Rainier or Olympic peak is added. Buildings, boats, dock details and nighttime window lights are simplified and illustrative.

## References inspected

- City of Kirkland, [Marina Park](https://www.kirklandwa.gov/Government/Departments/Parks-and-Community-Services/Find-a-Park/Marina-Park), including its [pavilion photograph](https://www.kirklandwa.gov/files/assets/public/v/1/images/marinapark3retouched.jpg?w=1200): pavilion placement, curved seawall and shoreline boulders.
- David Schott, [Moss Bay, Kirkland](https://www.flickr.com/photos/dave_mcmt/31843815086/), photographed July 29, 2016: explicitly described by its photographer as a panorama from Marina Park across Moss Bay. Shows broad open water, the marina/pier to the left and the pavilion-side shore to the right.
- [Kirkland Moss Bay panorama](https://bitfabrik.io/blog/index.php?id=117), with [image](https://bitfabrik.io/blog/dateien/Kirkland-Moss-Bay/P1330474eq.jpg): developed waterfront, marina and distant low shore. Its orange annotation and people are not part of the new illustration.
- Explore Kirkland, [Marina Park](https://www.explorekirkland.com/directory/marina-park/): official tourism context identifying the beach, pavilion and Lake Washington/Seattle views.

Reference photo bytes remain in ignored local research artifacts and are not included in the application or review deliverable. The generated art synthesizes the geographic features in the previously approved original watercolor treatment. Exact built-in image-generation/editing prompts are in `prompts.json`; the night canvas was derived from the new day canvas to retain the same geometry.

The JPEG filenames are unchanged for compatibility with the existing resolver. Their skies remain intentionally empty: sun/moon, stars, cloud, fog and precipitation stay in the original separate static layers. Both the information layout and weather/day-night behavior remain unchanged. The backdrop is a fixed personal illustration; the app still uses the forecast location and device coordinates for weather data.
